# Scoped prior-art and validation review

Research date: 2026-10-07. Crossref bibliographic searches and accessible publisher abstracts were reviewed for aluminum/ceramic heat-spreader and hybrid composites. Queries included “Al SiC AlN hybrid metal matrix thermal expansion composite,” “aluminium nitride reinforced aluminum metal matrix composite thermal expansion,” and “Al SiC interface Al4C3 water reaction thermal expansion.” An initial Al/SiC/B4C pool was also checked; B4C was subsequently omitted because the accessible supplier source lacked a complete verified property set.

The families are established. This study proposes a model-selected recipe within them, without synthesizing or experimentally validating it. The search does not establish whether an exact recipe, microstructure or processing route is new, and does not constitute an exhaustive patent search.

| Reference | Evidence reviewed | Consequence for this study |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [“Aluminum-Matrix Aluminum Nitride Particle Composite as a Low-Thermal-Expansion Thermal Conductor” (1993)](https://doi.org/10.1557/proc-323-207) | Crossref abstract reports pressure-assisted vacuum infiltration; 59 vol% AlN gave conductivity 157 W/(m·K) and CTE 9.8 × 10⁻⁶/K over 35–100°C. | Low-CTE Al/AlN has experimental prior art. This different-loading specimen is a comparator, not validation of this model. |
| [“Optimizing Tribological Performance of AA5128/SiC/AlN Hybrid Metal Matrix Nanocomposites by Taguchi Technique” (2023)](https://doi.org/10.17756/nwj.2023-s3-176) | Crossref title and publication metadata only; publisher/PDF returned HTTP 503. | Bibliographic evidence of an Al-alloy/SiC/AlN hybrid. Performance and methods were not verified. |
| [“Stable and metastable phase equilibria in the chemical interaction between aluminium and silicon carbide” (1990)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01045395) | Publisher abstract describes a pure Al/SiC phase study with an Al₄C₃-producing reaction at 923 ± 3 K. | Processing can change the assumed phase inventory; perfect inert-mixture behavior cannot be guaranteed. |
| [“Thermal conductivity of SiC fine particles reinforced Al alloy matrix composite with dispersed particle size” (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1632022) | Crossref abstract covers 10–40 vol% SiC; conductivity and interfacial conductance decreased as SiC loading increased. | Stiffness and CTE proxies do not establish measured conductivity; particle size and interfaces require testing. |
| [“Investigation of Mechanical Properties of Aluminium 6061-Silicon Carbide, Boron Carbide Metal Matrix Composite” (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12633-016-9479-8) | Publisher abstract describes stir-cast Al6061/SiC/B4C and mechanical tests. | Earlier considered B4C hybrid family is also established. |

The next evidence should come from matched-processing specimens: pure Al; the proposed hybrid; and each binary control at the same total ceramic volume fraction. Verify actual density, porosity and phase fractions, particle dispersion, cracking and reaction layers using microscopy and diffraction. Measure elastic modulus and Poisson ratio while distinguishing dynamic from static tests. Measure thermal conductivity and CTE over a common declared temperature interval, including repeated thermal cycles and residual strain. Strength, toughness, fatigue and long-term interface stability require their own measurements.

No processing prescription is validated here. After characterization, replace the bulk-grade proxies with measured powder/matrix/composite evidence, model interfaces and porosity, and repeat the screen before recommending a qualified product or claiming a new material.
